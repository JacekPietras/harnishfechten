# 2025 *In Arms et Sine Arms* Harnischfechten — event account

> **Status:** Secondary account of an event, not an official published ruleset. The author says the rules were provided verbally and notes that some details may have been misunderstood. Use it as an example of format and judging choices, not as authoritative wording.

## Source

- Grauenwolf, “Rules for Harnischfechten in HEMA,” published 23 September 2025, describing the 2025 WMA Workshop event *In Arms et Sine Arms*.
- [Event rules and judging account](https://grauenwolf.wordpress.com/2025/09/23/rules-for-harnischfechten-in-hema/)

## Event format

- Pas d’Armes-style event with five instructors defending the field.
- Each challenger selected a weapon (dagger, sword, poleaxe, or spear), chose barrier or open-field fighting, and could opt into sword mordhau and/or grappling.
- Daggers were allowed as secondary weapons regardless of the chosen primary weapon.
- Each challenge consisted of three passes, unless a fight-ending condition ended it sooner.
- There was no overall winner; matches were evaluated individually.

## Scoring calls described

- A pass ended on a solid blow to any location with a poleaxe or a sword crossguard.
- A solid thrust with any weapon to a mail-protected location also ended a pass.
- Glancing blows did not count. Full-power blows were not required; controlled, accurate delivery was expected.
- Thrusts had to be held; a momentary touch followed by retreat did not count.
- A polearm haft could distract but did not score.
- Doubles were ties; the pass was not repeated. After-blows did not count.

## Fight-ending conditions and disarms

The account lists a strike to an unarmored location, a blow to the face of a perforated helmet, complete disarm, or being driven from the field as fight-ending conditions.

Complete disarm meant losing all weapons. Losing a primary weapon while retaining a dagger did not count as complete disarm. Lost weapons were not automatically returned between passes, although opponents generally allowed recovery except in cases of total disarm.

## Grappling and judging

- The event allowed grappling by prior choice. A clean throw was described in theory as a possible fight-ender, but completed throws were not used in the event format, so the account could not verify how that rule would work in practice.
- Two marshals called blows as they saw them. Participants could also self-call blows.
- Marshals could tap participants on the helmet to get their attention because helmets limited hearing.

## Notes for comparison

- This model uses pass-ending conditions and binary outcomes rather than cumulative wound-severity points.
- It combines judge calls with fighter self-calls, a policy that differs from the judge-only approach advocated in one of the local feedback documents.
- The source raises armor fit and gap coverage as factors affecting whether attacks reach openings, but it does not provide a detailed armor classification chart.

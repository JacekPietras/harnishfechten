# QLHF Harnischfechten Rules — reference summary

> **Status:** External reference summary, not an adopted Harnischfechten rule for this project. The source describes Queensland Living History Federation (QLHF) intergroup combat rules for 14th- and 15th-century fighting. Event organizers are expected to specify event-level rules; its scoring chart is explicitly a sample.

## Source

- Queensland Living History Federation, *Harnischfechten: Intergroup Combat Rules, 14th & 15th Century*, version 1, dated 7 March 2019.
- [Original PDF](https://www.qlhf.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/QLHF-Harnischfechten-Rules-v1-2019-03.pdf)

This file is a concise paraphrase for comparison and does not replace the source document.

## Purpose and format

The rules aim to present historically grounded armored combat safely for modern participants. They are primarily designed for points-based combat but are also intended to inform larger events. The rules emphasize that the event must explain its standards in advance and that participants should understand them before fighting.

## Armor categories and judging effect

- **Unprotected:** Areas not covered by the defined armor categories. The source also places perforated steel fencing-mask faces in this category. Any blow to an unprotected target is fair, subject to safety restrictions.
- **Light armor:** Mail, properly hardened leather, properly constructed padded garments, and similar protection. By default it stops cuts but is vulnerable to thrusts.
- **Heavy armor:** Steel or iron plate and properly overlapped small plates. By default it is proof against most blows; the weapon-versus-armor chart supplies the sample exceptions.
- Modern safety equipment that is historically inconsistent generally does not count as armor for scoring. Marshals decide whether armor is safe and suitable.
- The event announces armor and weapon requirements beforehand. The source gives special requirements for throat, neck, and helmet protection, including stronger helmets where percussive weapons are used.

## Fair blows and bout scoring

- Event organizers choose the number of fair blows required to win. The document gives **three to five** as a general range; it does not impose a single point scale.
- A fair blow needs form and clear intent, and must be capable of scoring against the armor category struck.
- Strikes are controlled: the attacker shows intent and accuracy while reducing force so the blow can be received safely.
- The sample weapon-versus-armor chart is reproduced in paraphrased form below. It is explicitly a default example, and event rules may differ.

| Weapon/action | Unprotected | Light armor | Heavy armor | Source qualification |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Dagger thrust | Scores | Scores | — | With the point |
| Dagger cut | Scores | — | — | With the edge |
| Sword thrust | Scores | Scores | — | With the point |
| Sword cut | Scores | — | — | With the edge |
| Sword handle | Scores | — | — | Pommel and quillons |
| Spear thrust | Scores | Scores | — | With the point |
| Spear cut | Scores | — | — | With the edge |
| Percussive weapon thrust | Scores | Scores | — | Point and butt spike |
| Percussive weapon cut/blow | Scores | Scores | Scores | Edge, hammer, and hook |

The chart assesses the armor at the location struck, not the combatant's overall armor level. The rules explain that armor's advantage is making a fair blow harder to land, rather than changing the number of blows needed to win.

## Invalid, prohibited, or restricted actions

The source's examples include:

- An opponent walking into a sword, or a weapon falling onto someone, does not qualify as a fair blow.
- A sword strike against plate does not count in the sample rules.
- Blows to the spine and groin are prohibited.
- Intentional attacks to unarmored face or throat, hands, and feet are prohibited.
- Careless or uncontrolled attacks can be referred to the safety marshal and may lead to a warning or removal.
- Thrusting upward and strikes to the side of the helmet are only considered when the event's armor standard supports them and event rules explicitly allow them.
- A yielding combatant should clearly announce the yield and disarm before leaving the field.
- Whether wrestling is allowed, and its limits, is left to the marshal/event rules.
- Shield hands must be protected. Shield aggression is restricted to fully armored areas; head contact should only show intent. Shield pushes are allowed, but running bashes are not.

## Officials and event procedure

- **Group Marshal:** Oversees training, authorizes weapons and armor against safety and authenticity standards, sets minimum standards for hosted events, and can remove people for unsafe practice.
- **Senior Marshal:** Oversees event safety, conducts pre- and post-fight briefings, checks equipment, and can stop combat or remove a participant for unsafe conduct.
- **Flag Marshal:** Supports safety officials and calls hits during point- or strike-based bouts.
- The guide suggests at least one marshal per four combatants, adding another marshal for each additional group of four.
- Combat begins on the marshal's call, traditionally “la celle alle,” signaled with a pole held between combatants. “Hold” requires immediate cessation. The marshal may use the pole to get participants' attention if needed.

## Potential comparison points for the local guide

- Armor categories may provide a simpler judge-facing model than reconstructing the exact effect of every armor layer.
- The QLHF chart distinguishes weapons and contact types, including sword pommel/quillon, pole-weapon heads, and hafts.
- It leaves event-specific decisions explicit: number of scoring blows, allowed helmet attacks, wrestling, and detailed equipment standards.
- Its concept of a “fair blow” combines technique, intent, target armor, and controlled delivery; this differs from a fixed wound-severity point scale.

## Context and judging model

Feldlager uses the rules to manage a multi-day, 15th-century live-action wargame involving units, cavalry, missiles, and battlefield objectives. The document explicitly says that it is not an ideal format for individuals to demonstrate competitive fighting skill.

The White Cell acts as event referees and game masters. In melee, participants track their own hit points and are expected to do so honestly; when uncertain, the rules tell them to take the hit. The system aims to keep participants active in the larger battle rather than decide a one-on-one bout by judges assigning wound-severity points.

## General hit conventions

- The full body is a target except the unarmored face and throat; unarmored arms and legs are valid targets.
- A simple touch to a valid target counts. Hard hits are not required and should be avoided.
- Every valid hit removes one hit point, and participants step back at least two metres after each hit.
- Participants typically have one to three hit points. Their casualty card determines what happens when those are gone (for example, being wounded, routed, or dead).
- For real injury, participants call “Medic!” and everyone stops and makes space. “Wounded!” is used for an in-game casualty to avoid confusion.

## Armor interaction

Armor types have standardized effects in this ruleset. For protection to count, the attack must land on the armor itself, not beside it.

| Protection | Effect in the Feldlager rules |
|---|---|
| Plate | Stops everything. A controlled, light downward touch to the helmet can count when made with a poleaxe, halberd, warhammer, or similar weapon. |
| Brigandine | Stops everything except firearms, crossbows, and horse lances. |
| Mail, heavy padding, hardened leather | Stops cuts, but not firearms, crossbows, lances, or thrusts from hand weapons. |
| Unarmored areas, including clothing and skin | Stops nothing; any touch counts as a hit. |

## Weapons and safety details relevant to comparison

- The rules allow fully blunted steel training weapons with rounded or thickened tips, and polearms with wooden shafts and rubber, plastic, or wooden heads. Daggers must be blunt and not sharply pointed; night operations permit only wooden or rubber daggers.
- The face should not be targeted, even behind a visor. Thrusts should be directed away from the face and from a defined central upper-chest “V” area toward the clavicles, to reduce the risk of a thrust slipping into the face.
- Downward helmet strikes are allowed only as light, controlled touches; if the strike cannot be delivered lightly, it should not be made.
- A cavalry lance touch to a vulnerable target counts for three hit points and immediately makes the fighter a casualty. This is a large-battle game convention, not a suggested duel score.

## What may be useful for the judge guide

- The rules offer a concise example of armor categories and weapon-versus-armor effects.
- They make the distinction between a valid target and the armor actually covering that target explicit: armor protection applies only when the strike lands on it.
- They separate a real emergency stop command from in-game casualty language.
- Their “simple touch” standard and self-tracked hit points are designed for chaotic melee play. They should not be imported into a one-on-one judge-scored format without deliberate adaptation.

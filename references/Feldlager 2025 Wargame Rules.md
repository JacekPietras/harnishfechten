## What these rules are (and what they aren’t)

Feldlager 2025 is a Live Action Wargame that draws on aspects of historical reenactment, historical European martial arts (HEMA) studies, military simulation (MILSIM), and live action role play (LARP) to create an environment where we can attempt to recreate and experience elements of 15th century European warfare. Our priority is to provide a safe and fun environment to explore interpretations of historically-based formations, maneuvers, and wartime activities.

These rules seek to accomplish this by promoting longer engagements that avoid the “everyone dies in 30 seconds” problem and which allow commanders on the field the opportunity to make more interesting and deliberate tactical decisions before units are wiped out. Our casualty card system is designed to promote a more historical distribution of casualties on the field as well, all while keeping as many participants active on the field as long as possible. Our approach to weapons, troop types, and armor should also provide the opportunity to explore combined arms engagements, including infantry, cavalry, and missile troops in ways that act at least a little bit like we believe they did in the 15th Century.

These rules aren’t perfect, however, and they can’t be everything all at once. We’ve made compromises for simplicity (and the rules are complicated enough despite that), scale, and the different backgrounds of our participants such as HEMA, Reenactment, and LARP, each of whom are accustomed to different conventions and levels for intensity in group combats. These rules aren’t a perfect simulation of medieval warfare and they don’t provide an ideal environment for individual combatants to competitively show off their skills.

We ask as you read and apply these rules at Feldlager that you work to embrace the spirit of what we’re trying to do here by working as a military unit, treating your “life” as valuable and avoiding silly heroics, and having fun by safely embracing the role of a 15th century European warfighter for a few days.

## Participant Types

The organization for running the Feldlager war game is largely based on modern military exercises. In this type of war game, there are three main types of participants:

### Blue Team, Red Team

Blue and Red teams are the two sides of the conflict. Most players belong to one of these two teams. Blue team normally has training objectives (in the case of Feldlager, interpretations of historical manuals that we wish to test), while Red Team acts as an oppositional force for the purposes of creating an environment for Blue Team to test its training objectives.

Both Red and Blue Teams operate dynamically, with some guidance but little to no scripting. Both Red and Blue Team report to their respective commanders, who receive guidance from the White Cell throughout the war game. Players assigned to either Red or Blue teams may not change side or allegiance without specific instructions from the White Cell. Players should not intentionally act against the interest of their assigned teams (e.g., by being double agents, switching sides, etc.) without specific instructions from the White Cell. Players who cannot follow this rule will be asked not to participate.

There is no pre-conceived winner or outcome for any of the exercises or engagements during the war game, unless directed otherwise by the White Cell as part of the training objectives.

### Green Team

The Green Team includes neutral or unaligned parties. Only the Green Team has the ability to change sides in play without specific instructions from the White Cell. Please note that there may be no Green Team in play.

### White Cell

The White Cell are the referees and “Game Masters” for the war game. They provide orders, guidance, guardrails, and injects into the wargame to keep it moving, on track, and productive. White cells also set respawn points and perform administrative movements (i.e., “out of play”) movements on the field to reposition units, set the board for play, or for safety reasons.

### Scoring (This Is A Game)

There is an element of friendly competition at Feldlager, meant to encourage players and commanders to work in the best interest of their teams throughout the main events of the weekend.

This year Feldlager has approximately six core events, each of which is scored separately based on the objectives given by the White Cell to the Red and Blue Commanders. The scores impact how each side fares in the end and determine the overall winners and losers of the weekend. The events for 2025 include a mass battle, small unit patrols, defending/assaulting a wagenburg, a castle siege and assault, and various night operations.

Although the exact scoring rubric is intentionally secret, on average each team will have the opportunity to score between 5 and 15 points per event. Scores (and scoring actions) will be shared at the end of the event.

Players are expected to act honorably, do nothing in the “metagame” that might prevent another unit from honestly attempting their objectives, and to strive to achieve their own objectives in character and aligned to the spirit of the event. Units that cheat will be penalized with negative scores and the leaders and soldiers who acted dishonorably will be asked to leave the event.

## Combat: Victory or Defeat

In an engagement, two or more units enter combat, delivering and receiving hits. Fighters who are hit lose hit points, and when a fighter loses all of their hit points they become casualties (see Casualty Cards, below). The goal of an engagement is to defeat enemy units by forcing them to retreat or route, or by destroying them.

### Destroyed Units

A unit is destroyed when all its members have become casualties and their banner has been taken. A destroyed unit cannot reform until both the destroyed unit and the unit that took its banner return to camp.

Unit leaders should avoid allowing their unit being destroyed, and should attempt to retreat in an orderly fashion if they lose half or more of their unit to any form of casualty in an engagement.

Destroyed Units should report to the nearest White-Cell respawn point (a white banner) for instructions, and may return to camp under a white banner (so that other units know they’re know it play).

### Retreating and Routed Units

A unit is disengaged when it has moved more than 50 meters away from any enemies, either voluntarily (retreating, retiring) or through casualties (routed, wiped out).

### Respawning and Respawn Points

Respawn points will be designated by the white cell, marked with a numbered red or blue banner. These marked respawn points are the only game-legal respawn points. Units may not stay within 30 meters of a respawn point of any color. No “camping” is allowed.

Casualties respawn at the respawn point when under the following conditions:

1. There is an officer present (Rottmeister, Fählein leader, Gewalthauff leader, Captain, etc.). The officer does not need to be a casualty themselves.

2. There are at least 3 troops returning with the officer. These can be a mixture of casualty and non-casualty troops.

You cannot “solo respawn” and return to your unit without an officer and at least 3 troops.

### Cavalry Respawns

Cavalry also respawn at marked respawn points, just like infantry.

1. Cavalry may respawn at their respawn point without an officer.

2. There must be at least 3 cavalry together to respawn.

### Respawn Points as Objectives

During the Patrols phase (Saturday Morning), Respawn points will also act as starting points for the action and as checkpoints for objectives that each unit will have. This may require a blue unit to approach a red respawn point or vice-versa. As a reminder, no “camping” any respawn point of any color is allowed, and no unit may “hold” within 30 meters of a respawn point.

### Rallying Around the Unit Banner

A retreating or routed infantry unit may rally around their banner no less than 30 meters from a respawn point and at least 50 meters from the nearest enemy force.

- Wounded and routed fighters may leave the respawn point (if it’s in a different place) and rejoin a rallying the unit in good health.

- Dead fighters may rejoin the unit as well, assuming there are no enemies within 50m.

- A unit that has lost its banner cannot rally by itself, but may join another unit that still has a banner.

### Lost Banners

A unit that loses its banner may not rally around their banner, but may rally around an ally’s banner.

Captured banners are returned immediately upon return to camp.

## Fighting and Casualty Cards

Before each major combat event (i.e., Patrols, Siege, Battle, etc.) each infantry participant will be issued a casualty card. You may receive several different cards over the course of a day, changing them out between events.

Each card contains three major pieces of information.

1. How many hit points you have.

2. What you do at the end of those hit points.

3. How to rejoin your unit after losing all of your hit points.

### Hit Points

- Participants will have between 1-3 hit points, as noted on their card. Cavalry always has 1 hit point.

- You lose a hit point every time you are hit by a weapon in a valid target (see Penetration & Armor, below).

- You must step to the back at least 2 meters every time you are hit, regardless of how many hit points you have. This is important!

- Participants will track their own hit points and be on their honor to do so as honestly and accurately as possible within the chaos of the melee. When in doubt, take the hit.

### Becoming a Casualty

When you lose all of your hit points you become a casualty and follow the instructions on your casualty card. When you become a casualty, one of three things happens:

1. Dead. You (pretend to) die. Fall to the ground in a safe place and stay there, taking reasonable precautions for your own safety and that of those around you. This will usually require moving to the side of the engagement, especially if there are horses about.

2. Wounded! You (pretend that you are) are gravely wounded. Fall to the ground, roll around, call for your comrades to save you. To prevent confusion for real injuries, shout “Wounded!” between your cries for help. Shouting that you are injured or hurt will be treated as if you are really hurt and we don’t want to confuse medical staff. If you are struck again while on the ground or while being escorted back to the respawn point you are Dead. You can limp to the respawn point if aided by a healthy fighter.

3. Rout! You turn and flee the battlefield to the respawn point. If you are struck after you have left the line on your way to your regroup point, you are Dead.

Please note that while the rules cover killing the wounded, it’s bad form and outside of historical practice for most wars and it doesn’t improve your unit's score in any way. We recommend units avoid killing the enemy wounded in most situations. That said, if you are killed while wounded, be a good sport about it!

### How to rejoin the combat

1. Wounded fighters can be escorted by any non-wounded fighter to the respawn point. Once at the respawn point they can return to combat if joined by an Officer and 2 or more other troops. The officer and other troops do not also need to be casualties themselves, though they are allowed to be.

2. Routed fighters go to the respawn point of their own accord but cannot escort the wounded. Once at the respawn point they can return to combat if joined by an Officer and 2 or more other troops. The officer and other troops do not also need to be casualties themselves, though they are allowed to be.

3. All Fighters (Dead, Wounded, and Routed) can rejoin their unit at its banner after the engagement is over (i.e., when there are no enemy units within 50m).

See “Rallying Around the Unit Banner” and "Respawning and Respawn Points” for more.

### Sample Casualty Card

WOUNDED! 2 HP This Card is Secret. Do not show this card to anyone but the White Cell until you run out of Hit Points (HP). When you lose all your HP You (pretend that you are) are gravely wounded. Shout out that you are wounded. Find a safe place nearby to sit down.

If you are struck again you are Dead.

How to Rejoin Your Unit You may be escorted by an unwounded ally back to the respawn point. You respawn and rejoin the unit when joining an officer and at least 2 other troops, or when the unit rallies at the banner at the end of an engagement.

### What if you are hurt for real?

Safety is the most important priority on the field.

In the event you or someone near you sustains a real injury during an engagement, the injured party or another person nearby should shout “Medic!” Upon hearing “Medic” everyone in the engagement should immediately stop what they are doing and create space for the injured party to be helped and, if necessary, evacuated from the area.

Once the injured party has been safely evacuated from the engagement area, the unit commanders should meet, briefly discuss how to re-initiate the engagement, and then do so.

If you are wounded in play (i.e., due to the results of your casualty card), do NOT shout “Medic.” Say “Wounded!” instead.

### Other instructions for your casualty card

- Keep your card until the White Cell or your Fähnlein leader collects it and gives you a new one.

- Do not lose your card. Put it in your helmet lining, under your hat, in your purse, etc.

- Keep your card secret, do not share it with anyone until after you’ve run out of hit points, at which point anyone can ask to see it.

- Do not trade your card with any fighters.

- Memorize your hit points and what you do when you lose all of your hit points so you don’t have to pull your card out during the battle.

- Perform the card-directed action immediately after losing your last hit point and taking your two steps back.

- Remember that safety is more important than the actions the card gives you. Only perform the card-directed action when safe for you and the people or horses around you.

- Return your card to your Fähnlein leader or white cell member when requested. (Note: The White Cell are the event organizers and “game masters.”)

### Casualties for Cavalry and Castle Defenders

Casualty cards are not used for Cavalry, Castle Defenders, or Night Operations.

- Cavalry always have 1 HP and always Rout

- Castle defenders always have 2 HP and are always Wounded. There are no respawn points inside of the castle, so wounded castle defenders are out of play until the siege is over.

- There are no HP in night operations. Guards killed with daggers should die and sit quietly for about 15 minutes, then head back into camp. Spies and other operatives should surrender when confronted out loud with a guard who sees them and could reasonably strike them with a polearm. Note that for safety reasons, no striking with anything but a rubber or wooden dagger is allowed during night operations.

## Rules for Weapons and Armor

### Penetration & Armor

Details on targets and safety are published in the Rules of Engagement videos on https://feldlager.eu/videos. This is an incomplete reiteration of some of those points, with additional and updated information on equipment standards. If information here and in the videos conflicts, use the information here.

- The full body is a target, except for the unarmored face and throat.

- Unarmored limbs, including arms and legs, are valid targets.

- All attacks count at a simple touch. Hard hits are not required and should be avoided.

### Weapon Standards

Reenactment and “HEMA” style steel training weapons with a historical profile are allowed on the field. This includes but may not be limited to:

- Steel weapons (swords, daggers, pole arms, axes, etc.) that are fully blunted and have rounded or otherwise thickened tips. No sharp tips or edges allowed.

- Polearms with wooden shafts and rubber, plastic, or wooden-heads. Non-steel heads must be painted with silver paint. Staves functioning as spears may have black-painted tips.

- Daggers must be fully blunt and may not be sharply pointed. They may be tipped with rubber arrowhead points, leather, or similar. Wooden and (painted) plastic/rubber daggers are acceptable, assuming the handles look “real.”

- No sharp weapons of any kind, including eating knives or utility knives of any kind are allowed outside of camp or in any conflict areas.

- For night operations only wooden or rubber daggers are allowed.

### Minimum Armor Standards

All participants on the battlefield are expected to have a certain minimum degree of armor appropriate to the role they take on the battlefield.

- A steel helmet covering the top half of the head is the absolute minimum. No combatant can go without a helmet. If a non-combatants is on or near the combat area without a helmet, it should be very obvious that that non-combatant is not wearing a helmet, so that other combatants do not accidentally confuse that non-combatant for a combatant.

- The following protective equipment is recommended by not mandatory:

      - Steel gauntlets are encouraged for infantry engaging in hand-to-hand fighting.

      - Historical or modern groin protection (e.g., a cup) for fighters with testicles. If worn, Modern protection must worn under clothing and completely invisible to other participants.

      - Historical or modern throat protection. If worn, modern protection must be invisible to other participants.

### Striking Standards

Safety in attacking is of paramount importance. Attack safely, and interpret touches generously. If you think you’ve been hit, you’ve been hit.

- Fighters should not target the face, even if an opponent is wearing a visor or the face appears to be a safe target.

- Thrusts should be aimed away from the face and away from any target that might slide accidentally to the face. Imagine a “V” shape from the center of the chest up to the outer points of the clavicles; do not thrust within or above this V.

- Strikes down upon the helmet are allowed, but must be made in a controlled manner, hitting lightly. If the strike cannot be made lightly, do not make the strike.

### Special Weapons

- Cavalry Lances count as 3 hit points on a touch to a vulnerable target (i.e., anything but plate). A fighter touched by a cavalry lance in a valid target becomes a casualty immediately.

- Firearms must be used in Volley Fire against a massed unit (i.e., Fähnlein or Rotte). All fighters with 1 hit point in a unit targeted by a firearm volley become casualties immediately. This includes fighters who may have started with more hit points, but currently have only 1 hit point left.

- Crossbows and Self Bows may be shot at a cavalryman's valid targets from up close as normal. When using volley shot (5 or more simultaneous shots) against a Cavalry unit, aim the shots in front of (not at) the cavalry. The Cavalry has an obligation to retreat 100m or to the nearest respawn point (but may immediately return to combat).

In order to keep the game fairly simple, all armor of a given type behaves the same way every time. For an armor’s defensive quality to apply, the attack or touch must land on the armor (not next to it):

- Plate stops everything. Controlled, downward touches to the helmet count if performed with a poleaxe, halberd, warhammer, or similar.

- Brigandine stops everything but guns, crossbows, and horse lances

- Mail, heavy padding, and hardened leather stop cuts, but not guns, crossbows, lances or thrusts from hand weapons.

- Unarmored areas (clothing, skin) stop nothing--any touch to those targets counts as a hit.

## Planned Unit Structure

In the field, there are two armies: Blue Team and Red Team. Each Army is divided up into Gewalthaufen of Infantry and Conroi Cavalry. Each Gewalthauf is made up of 3-8 Banners, or Fähnlein, led by a Banner Leader. Each Fähnlein is made up of 2-3 Rotte, led by a Rottmeister. Each Rotte has 8-12 fighters.

The Cavalry are divided into Conroi (Squadrons) of 3 Chambres, each of which is divided into two lances of 4 cavalry or a light cavalry chambre of 6-7. The Blue Team Hauptmann (Captain) is Jake Norwood, who will lead the overall Army and the Gewalthauf. Arne Koets will lead the Cavalry for the Blue Team. The Red Team Captains will be announced at a later date. Fähnlein (Banner) Leaders and Rottmeisters will be drawn from the attendees, with an attempt to keep leaders of groups of 8-15 as Rottmeisters and leaders of groups over 20 as Banner Leaders. Chambre and Lance leaders will also be drawn from the attendees and named at a later date.

Captains are responsible for the overall strategy and orders for the patrols, siege, and battles. Fähnlein and Chambre (Banner) Leaders report to the Gewalhauf/Conroi Captains and are responsible for the execution of their Captain’s orders and the care and deployment of their troops. Rottmeisters/Lance Leaders report to their Banner Leaders and assist in the command and control of the unit.

On the field, the primary unit of maneuver is the Fähnlein or Conroi, which are represented by a Banner. Members of a Fähnlein must stay close to their Banner at all times unless they become casualties (see Casualty Cards, below). A Rotte can detach from its banner assuming it has a pennant to follow and stays within vocal command distance (approximately 50m), such as during a quick flanking action. Cavalry units may spread out further as required, but should generally keep together except when executing a maneuver.
